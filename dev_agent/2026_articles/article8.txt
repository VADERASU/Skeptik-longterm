The Push for HPV Vaccination Despite Concerns

The HPV vaccine is heavily promoted for 11- and 12-year-olds (both boys and girls) as a way to “prevent” infection with strains of Human Papillomavirus (HPV), linked to certain cancers. Recently, the FDA expanded Gardasil’s approval to include adults ages 27–45, despite evidence that prior exposure to HPV can increase the risk of illness or cancer after vaccination.

1 — Most People Will Clear HPV Naturally
HPV is common: about 80% of sexually active women contract it by age 50. However, the vast majority (about 91%) clear it naturally without medical intervention. [1] So, why do 9% not clear it? The answer likely lies in immune system health. HPV may be more of an opportunistic infection in those who are immunocompromised rather than a direct cause of cancer in healthy people.

So, rather than focusing on a mass vaccination program for everyone, we could do more by looking for and correcting the causes of immune weakness. We could focus on enhancing our ability to fight off all infections and reduce all cancer risks without the known harms of the vaccine.

2 — The HPV Vaccine Can Cause Serious Health Issues
Does it make sense to get a vaccine to prevent an illness that 91% of us can clear on our own when the vaccine can cause more serious diseases? Japan stopped recommending the HPV vaccine years ago due to safety concerns.

One reason was that the vaccine contains an aluminum adjuvant used to “enhance the effectiveness,“ but it only provokes a stronger immune response because it’s toxic. Aluminum has no beneficial role in human physiology, and injected aluminum is linked to autoimmune disorders, neurological damage, and chronic illness.

Years of studies have shown the harm vaccines can cause as they trigger immune problems. But because U.S. law shields vaccine makers from liability, these harms often go unaddressed. [2][3]

3 — Flawed “Placebo” in Safety Testing of These Vaccines
Knowing what we know about the harm of aluminum adjuvants, using aluminum as a “placebo” in safety trials is not only unscientific—it’s unethical. Yet that’s exactly what happened. In Gardasil’s safety studies, one group received the full vaccine, while the so‑called placebo group was injected with the same aluminum adjuvant found in the vaccine. [3]

This sleight of hand all but guaranteed the results would downplay harm. Since aluminum is already linked to serious health problems—including autoimmune disorders and neurological damage—both groups were exposed to a toxic ingredient. Predictably, the studies reported only a slight difference in adverse events between the two groups.

5 — Increased Risk of Other HPV Strains
There are over 100 HPV strains, with about 13 considered high-risk. The original vaccine covered just four strains; the current version covers nine.

One study found that women who received the original vaccine had a 40% higher chance of contracting a high-risk HPV strain not included in the vaccine than those who had not been vaccinated. So, rather than reassessing the approach, the solution offered was… another vaccine. [5][6]

Studies show that vaccinated women were not only more likely to contract other high‑risk strains not covered by the shot, but many were also infected with the very strains the vaccine was designed to prevent.

Since Gardasil was introduced in 2006, rates of cervical, throat, and anal cancers have risen—not fallen. Before 2006, these cancers were declining. This reversal suggests the vaccine has not delivered on its promises. The vaccine that they will guilt you into taking is doing nothing to protect children.
